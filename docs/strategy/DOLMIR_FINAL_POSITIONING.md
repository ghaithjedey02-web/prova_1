# DOLMIR — Final Commercial Positioning

**Date:** 2026-08-29
**Status:** Decision document. Supersedes the positioning in
`04-beachhead-decision.md` and `05-offer-and-pricing.md`. The vertical choice

## 1. Executive conclusion

**The new hypothesis fails as stated, and succeeds as an operating principle.**

"Tool-agnostic AI/process engineering for Italian SMEs" is not a position. It is
a procurement virtue. No owner in Brescia has ever wanted a tool-agnostic
partner. Worse, it is almost verbatim what Yellow Tech, Arko, CRMpartners and
Giallo already publish — adopting it walks us *into* our nearest competitor
rather than away from them.

What survives is the discipline underneath it: measure before you build,
integrate with what exists, be willing to say "buy the product." Those are how
we work. They are not what we sell.

**What we actually sell is office throughput in a manufacturing SME.**

The positioning that is both true and unoccupied:

> **Il collo di bottiglia non è in officina.**
> A decade of Industria 4.0 incentives modernised the shop floor. The office
> did not move. In a 30-person manufacturer, two or three people now read,
> interpret, retype and answer everything — and they are the limit on the
> whole company.

That is specific, recognisable, Italian, and no competitor owns it. Uptool and
Spanflug sell a quoting tool. Yellow Tech sells AI transformation across six
sectors. Giallo removes repetitive work for SMEs and professional practices in
general. Nobody is addressing the office-versus-production asymmetry inside
manufacturing SMEs as the thing they fix.

**Three consequential changes:**

1. **Stop being a quoting company.** RFQ→preventivo becomes our first workflow,
   not our identity. Identity = the office. Quoting is contested by ten
   products; "the office can't keep up" is contested by nobody.
2. **Kill the separate paid pilot.** "AI pilot purgatory" is a documented
   Italian failure mode. Fold the accuracy gate into the implementation
   contract instead. This is differentiated *and* it fixes a real disease.
3. **Cut the retainer to €390–790/month.** Market evidence puts maintenance at
   €150–200/month. €600 as an entry tier is indefensible.

**And the sharpest risk, stated up front:** after three Rilievi we may discover
the honest recommendation is nearly always "buy Spanflug." If so DOLMIR is a
€5k configuration shop, not a €25k implementation business. That is the
existential question, and §22 makes it the first thing we test.

## 2. What DOLMIR actually sells

Not AI. Not automation. Not agents.

**We sell the ability to handle more commercial and administrative work without
hiring another person for the office — installed, working, and measured against
your own numbers.**

The owner's version, in one sentence:
> *"Vi facciamo evadere più richieste con le persone che avete già."*

Three things make that a product rather than a promise:
- It is **installed**, not advised. A consultant leaves a report; we leave a working system.
- It is **inside your systems**. It works with the gestionale you bought in 2008.
- It is **measured**. We install the measurement, so you check us rather than trust us.

## 4. Buyer

**The titolare.** In a 10–80 person Italian manufacturer, the person who feels
the pain, controls the budget and answers the phone is one person. That single
fact is worth more to a company with no track record than any market-size number.

- Typically 45–65, technical background, often second generation
- Has personally been cold-called by three "digital transformation" vendors this year
- Has been burned, or knows someone who was, by software that was bought and abandoned
- Reads a website for **under a minute** before deciding whether to reply
- Does not care about AI. Cares about orders, margin and whether things go out on time

**Secondary influencer:** the *preventivista* or office manager — the person
whose day the system changes. They cannot buy, but they can kill a project by
not using it. The demo must respect them, never imply they are being replaced.

## 5. Core problem

**The office is the constraint, and the owner cannot hire out of it.**

Three forces, all evidenced:

1. **The shop floor got modernised, the office didn't.** A decade of 4.0
   incentives went into machinery. The commercial and technical office is still
   two or three people with Excel and a mail client.
2. **Every enquiry requires judgement.** Made-to-order work cannot be handled by
   a template. Reading a request, deciding whether to bid, remembering what a
   similar job cost — that is skilled work, and it does not scale by hiring
   clerks.
3. **Hiring is blocked.** The skills are scarce and the cost is real; a
   fully-loaded office hire is roughly €35–45k/year. And Istat records that the
   #1 barrier to AI adoption in Italian firms is **lack of in-house skills
   (58.6%)** — so the owner cannot solve it internally either.

The consequences the owner actually notices: quotes going out late or not at
all, orders lost to whoever answered first, pricing that lives in one person's
head, and everything stopping when that person is on holiday.

## 7. Vertical decision

**Answer: A — stay with manufacturing SMEs.** With one correction: we widen from
*one workflow* to *the office function*, while keeping the sector narrow.

**Why not B (narrower).** The addressable pool is already about 3,000–5,000
firms in Lombardia (from 33,800 mechatronics local units). One person running
outbound needs 120–150 contacts a quarter. Narrowing to, say, only turning shops
would drop the pool toward the point where a bad quarter has no recovery. Over-
narrowing is the more likely error here than under-narrowing.

**Why not C (broader positioning).** Broad positioning is precisely what makes
Giallo, Arko and Yellow Tech interchangeable. "AI e automazione per le PMI" is
the most crowded sentence in this market. Our only structural advantage over
better-funded competitors is knowing one sector's work in detail.

**Why not D (abandon).** Nothing in the research argues against the sector. The
Phase 1 error was the competitive read on *one workflow*, not the choice of
customer. Uptool raising $6M from Khosla and Bessemer to serve exactly these
firms is evidence the customer is right, not wrong.

**The refinement that matters:** the segment is defined by *operational
situation* — a small office carrying a high-variety workload — not by ATECO
code. That definition travels: it holds for a machining shop, and it will hold
later for a plastics moulder or a food producer. Sector second, situation first.

## 8. Service architecture

Four stages. The whole model has to be explainable in under a minute:

> *Facciamo una chiamata. Se ha senso, misuriamo il vostro processo per due
> settimane e vi diamo i numeri. Se i numeri lo giustificano, costruiamo il
> sistema. Poi lo teniamo funzionante.*

### Stage 0 — Chiamata · free · 25 minutes
**Client buys:** nothing.
**We deliver:** six questions about their process and an honest opinion,
including "you don't need us."
**Why it exists:** everyone else offers a free call. Charging for a phone call
from an unknown company is a lost argument. This stage qualifies, it does not sell.
**Measured:** RFQ volume, who prepares quotes, time per quote, arrival-to-sent time.

### Stage 1 — **Rilievo di Processo** · €2,900 fixed · 2 weeks
*Rilievo* is the Italian industrial word for the survey you do before designing
an intervention. It signals depth and it is not the word competitors use for
their free "AI audit."

**Client buys:** measured facts about their own operation.
**We deliver:**
- The as-is process, documented, with measured cycle times
- Analysis of their **last 50 real requests** — volume, channel, response time, outcome where known
- Where the hours actually go, quantified
- Integration feasibility against their actual gestionale
- A fixed-price implementation proposal, **or a recommendation not to proceed**
- 90-minute findings session

**Why it exists:** it converts a €25k decision into a €2,900 decision, and it
produces the ROI model that justifies the implementation. The audit sells the project.
**The deliverable stands alone** — they keep the process map and the numbers
whether or not they continue. Fully credited against implementation within 60 days.

### Stage 2 — **Implementazione** · €18,000–35,000 · 6–10 weeks
**Client buys:** a working system inside their existing environment.
**We deliver:** intake, extraction, confidence gating, triage, retrieval from
their own history, draft generation, human approval workflow, gestionale
integration, audit trail, the measurement dashboard, training, 30 days hypercare.

**The accuracy gate — this replaces the pilot.** Before full build, we verify
field-level accuracy on their historic documents against a threshold written
into the contract. Below threshold, the project stops and they pay only the work
done to that point.

**Why no separate paid pilot:** "AI pilot purgatory" — pilots that never reach
production — is a named failure mode in the Italian market. Selling one is
selling the disease. A contractual gate gives the same risk reduction without
creating a stage that can become the destination.

### Stage 3 — **Presidio** · €390–790/month · 12 months then rolling
**Client buys:** the system continuing to work.
**We deliver:** hosting, monitoring, monthly accuracy review and retuning, up to
4h/month of changes, quarterly review against the measured baseline.
**Why it exists, honestly:** extraction accuracy decays as their customers change
document formats. Without active tuning the system silently degrades and they
churn angry. Say exactly that — it is true and it is defensible.

**Expansion, existing clients only:** conferma d'ordine → gestionale (€8–14k),
supplier RFQ (€10–18k), non-conformity handling (€12–20k). Each reuses most of
the engine.

### The white space

Two axes that matter: **how deep into the client's real environment you go**
(× axis) versus **how specific to their actual work you are** (y axis).

- Products: high specificity to the *task*, near-zero depth into the *environment*
- AI agencies: medium depth, low specificity to the sector
- ERP vendors: high depth, low specificity to the workflow
- Freelancers: low, low

**Empty quadrant: high environment depth × high work specificity.** Someone who
knows what an Italian machining office does all day *and* will get inside a
2008 gestionale to fix it. That is DOLMIR.

## 11. Why customers would choose DOLMIR

1. **We start from their documents, not a slide deck.** The Rilievo analyses 50 of their real requests.
2. **We work inside the gestionale they already have** — the thing every product avoids promising.
3. **We are specific to their work.** "Preventivista", "conto terzi", "su disegno" is not vocabulary a generalist fakes.
4. **We install the measurement**, so they verify instead of trusting.
5. **A human approves every output** — a decision-support tool, not an autonomous system making commercial commitments.
6. **We will tell them to buy a product instead**, and there is a page on the site that proves we mean it.
7. **The first commitment is €2,900**, not €25,000.

## 12. Why customers would NOT choose DOLMIR

Stated plainly, because pretending otherwise wastes our time:

1. **We have no clients.** No logos, no references, no case studies. For a conservative buyer this is often decisive and no amount of design fixes it.
2. **We are more expensive than a product.** €18k against $195/month is a comparison we lose on price and must win on outcome.
3. **We are one person.** Bus factor. Some buyers will ask, and the honest answer is uncomfortable.
4. **We are slower than a freelancer.** Six to ten weeks against "two weeks."
5. **They may not believe the office is the problem.** Many owners locate all cost on the shop floor.
6. **Their gestionale may be genuinely closed**, making integration expensive or impossible.
7. **They may be right to do nothing.** Below ~10 requests a week the economics do not work, and we should say so.

## 13. Moat / defensibility

**Honest starting position: at zero clients we have no moat.** Anything claimed
otherwise is theatre. What we have is a positioning nobody occupies, which is a
head start, not a defence.

**What is not a moat:**
- The technology — commodity, and a $195/month product does more
- The methodology — every competitor runs the same 4–5 phases; it is table stakes
- Tool-agnosticism — a policy anyone can adopt tomorrow
- The website — copyable in a fortnight

**What becomes a moat, built by clients 1–10:**
1. **Measured baselines across shops.** After ten Rilievi we know what normal looks like — average quote turnaround, RFQ volume per head, where hours go. Nobody else has that dataset for Lombard machining. It makes every subsequent sales conversation sharper and is publishable as authority.
2. **Integration knowledge.** What it actually takes to get data in and out of Zucchetti, TeamSystem, Dylog and bespoke gestionali. Expensive to learn, invisible from outside, exactly what stops products.
3. **Switching cost inside the account.** Their configured pricing logic and quote history live in a system we maintain. Not lock-in by design — a consequence of doing the work.
4. **Vocabulary and judgement.** Knowing which requests are worth quoting is domain knowledge that compounds and cannot be bought.

**Timeline: the moat starts existing at client three.** Everything before that is
execution speed.

## 15. Website strategy

**The site has one job:** a prospect who received our cold email searches DOLMIR,
lands here, and within 30 seconds thinks *"questi hanno capito il problema."*

It is not a lead-generation funnel and it is not a portfolio. It is the
credibility check that decides whether our email gets a reply.

**The 30-second test, in order:**

| Second | They must understand | Delivered by |
|---|---|---|
| 0–5 | What kind of company this is | Hero headline + eyebrow |
| 5–12 | That we understand *their* situation | The office-vs-production framing |
| 12–20 | What we actually do | Three-beat: misuriamo → riprogettiamo → consegniamo |
| 20–30 | Whether to believe us | The demonstration, and the comparison page |
| Exit | What happens next | One CTA |

**Three changes to what is currently built.** The design system, the
demonstration and the technical foundation stand — this is a copy and
information-architecture revision, not a rebuild.

1. **Reframe the home page** from "we build you a quoting system" to the office
   bottleneck. The hero changes; the structure mostly survives.
2. **Add `/confronto`** — the honest comparison page. See §18. This is the
   single highest-value addition available to us.
3. **Rebuild `/metodo` around measurement.** A 4–7 phase process is table stakes;
   presenting it as our differentiator is a weak claim a comparing buyer
   discounts. Make the *Rilievo deliverable* the hero of that page.

**Visual direction is unchanged and validated:** Italian, industrial, technical,
precise, expensive, controlled. Every effect must carry meaning. The Line's
human-approval gate already does exactly this and should stay.

## 17. Demo strategy

**Keep the RFQ demonstration.** It is the right first demo, for three reasons:

1. Quoting is the highest-value, highest-frequency office task in this segment.
2. It already demonstrates *judgement*, not just parsing — bid/no-bid triage
   against shop capability, retrieval of the shop's own history, the refusal to
   invent a price, the cross-customer warning.
3. It runs the real engine, offline, and cannot fail in a meeting room.

**But change what it is captioned to prove.**

- **Now:** "look what the AI can do."
- **Should be:** *"Guardate quante decisioni prende il vostro ufficio su una sola email."*

That reframe converts the demo from a technology demonstration into evidence of
business understanding — which is what the new positioning requires.

**One addition worth making later** (not now): a panel showing what the system
*did not* do — the judgement calls it deliberately left to a person. Under the
new positioning that is as persuasive as the automation itself.

**And it stays valid if we implement someone else's product**, because it shows
the *shape of the work* — which is identical whether we build it or configure
Spanflug.

## 18. Trust strategy

We have zero clients. Trust has to come from things that can be checked.

**On the two proposed lines.** Both are directionally right and both are too
weak as written:
- *"Non vendiamo software. Prima capiamo se vi serve davvero."* — "prima
  capiamo" is vague, and any vendor can say it.
- *"Se un prodotto esistente risolve meglio il problema, ve lo diremo."* — a
  virtue claim from a stranger. Big-4 firms say this and nobody believes them.

**The problem with both: they claim integrity rather than demonstrating it.**

**Stronger, because it is falsifiable:**

> ### "Se vi conviene comprare un prodotto invece di chiamarci, sta scritto qui."
> — linking to `/confronto`

That is a claim the visitor can check in one click. The page is the asset; the
sentence is just the door.

**`/confronto` should contain**, named and honestly assessed: Uptool, Spanflug,
the Italian vertical products, what TeamSystem and Zucchetti already include —
and a plain statement of when buying one of them beats calling us. No
competitor in this market does this. For a company with no clients it is the
highest-leverage trust asset available, and it costs nothing but nerve.

**Supporting trust assets, in order of strength:**
1. The working demonstration on their own document
2. `/confronto`
3. The explicit "what we don't do" list — already built, keep it
4. Naming the measurement we install
5. A real person on `/studio` — still missing, still important
6. Publishing the sector benchmark once 20+ calls are done

**Never:** invented testimonials, client logos, case studies, ROI figures,
certifications, or employee counts.

## 20. First-customer strategy

**The website will not produce the first customer.** It converts attention that
outbound creates. Treat it as a credibility check, then stop polishing it.

**Reframe the goal: three Rilievi, not one implementation.**

A €2,900 decision is an order of magnitude easier to win than €25,000, and three
completed Rilievi produce the thing we most lack — evidence. With permission,
anonymised findings ("in un'officina da 34 addetti in provincia di Bergamo, il
tempo medio fra richiesta e offerta era X") convert "we have a method" into
"here is what we found." That is the fastest route from zero to credible.

**Sequence:**
1. Enrich the prospect database so it is actually contactable — still the
   blocking task from Phase 1
2. Ship the `/confronto` page and the home rewrite
3. 20 hand-written outbound contacts, demo as the hook
4. Target 3 Rilievi before pursuing any implementation
5. Publish the anonymised aggregate after Rilievo three
6. Convert the strongest Rilievo into implementation one

**Warm paths beat cold ones and should be exhausted first** — anyone who already
knows you, and any commercialista or ERP reseller who serves these firms.

**MECSPE (next edition ~March 2027) is too late for the first customer.** Plan
it for scale, not for now.

## 22. Falsification tests

Written in advance so we cannot retrofit the story.

| # | Test | Threshold | If it fails |
|---|---|---|---|
| **T1** | **Does DOLMIR have a €25k job at all?** After 3 Rilievi, how many concluded that a bespoke implementation beats buying a product? | **≥2 of 3** | The business is configuration + integration, not implementation. Restructure to €6–10k projects with a higher retainer, or become an implementation partner for Spanflug/Uptool. **Test this first.** |
